description (as many lines as you like; blank line = new paragraph):
  4.021 (intro to design) solo project on designing functional objects with
  design constraints: 2ft x 2ft sheet of aluminum and using only one piece.

  I wanted to make a desk organizer that I could place anywhere on my desk. Ideally it should
  work on anyone's desk too and gives the user autonomy about orientation.
  I started scoping out on paper how many dividers there should be and the bare minimum of
  area needed to act as a sufficient divider. I also decided to allow for four possible sides
  that would interface with desk or ground and still allow for full functionality.

  The first prototype was lasercut from cardboard and had rough geometric cut outs. There turned out to
  be some misalignment with all the dividers after they were bent, there was a lack of support on
  the overhang divider for one of the configurations. I used tape to mimic the rigidness of the
  aluminum and redid the CAD in Rhino with the adjustments: this involved sizing down the protruding
  dividers I identified, adding a tab and slot joint, and making slotted relief cuts at the bends for
  ease of manual bending.

  The second prototype was cut with the metal laser cutter but the tolerancing of the slotted relief cuts
  were not large enough and required less distance between each slot to allow for manual bending. This led
  to the last prototype where the bends were much more easier to make, and then I topped it off with
  a bright orange powder coat for a clean finish.
