description (as many lines as you like; blank line = new paragraph):
  I got really excited by my access to a metal laser cutter and decided to use it. I saw dogs made of sheet metal
  using very geometric folds but wanted to see how I could manipulate metal into more organic shapes.

  My first experiment was to make a scaly fish. This choice was not arbitray: the scales would serve as relief
  cuts so that a curvature could be manually bent and also the metal scales would look nice. I started out in Procreate
  to get a rough estimate the shape, cut it out quickly and tested the sizing of the fins, of the thickness it would be.
  With that information, I made adjustments in Illustrator to make it more symmetrical and consistent. I cut the first prototype
  and realized that ratio of size versus thickness was too small and so the bends were much more difficult to make and required
  too much force. I sized up by x1.5 and the fish came alive after that with the specific method of using hard eged tools to
  bend the scales as well.

  I later experimented with making three small dinosaurs. The initial sketches were modeled after a Brachiosaurus,
  a Stegosaurus, and a more cartoon version of a dinosaur sitting down. The initial sketches were then made into paper prototypes
  after cutting them out and I adjusted the shape of them in their constructed form. This new editied version was similarly edited further
  in Illustrator and then cut by the metal laser cutter. One issue that came up was a lack of relief cuts on one of the dinosaur tails
  which caused a breakage in the material due to multiaxial stress at that endpoint.

  In a later prototype, I would increase the number of relief cuts and distribute in the other axis for bending.
