  A birthday dress for my sister she asked me to design and photograph

description (as many lines as you like; blank line = new paragraph):
  My sister had an idea for a dress that was heavily inspired by fashions icons
  like Rhinanna. And I was up for the challenge to fabricate it and started sketching out the components
  she liked from other designs she saw online of the dress. The black satin was chosen for a slim and elegant look.
  The skirt needed to drape as per request of my sister and this all was done with one piece of
  fabric to reduce visible stitching. This process was a cycle of constantly testing the fabric
  with her, making measurements, and even practicing walking with the predicted train of the skirt.

  The top piece was much more difficult. One issue my sister outlined was a lack of support from other
  dresses in the chest area. Thus, a question of whether this should be made of satin entirely or two
  different materials was a creative decision up to me. For the structure of the dress, I experimented
  with making a corset for the first time. This involved more measurements to test durability, comfortability,
  and ensure for seam allowance. In total there were seven sowing patterns handmade by me.
  sowing patterns used. Unfortunately, the fit was slightly underfitted and did not provide the best coverage.

  This led to top piece number #2 seen in the first picture that is sown on to the skirt, also made of satin.
  More measurements were made but this couldn't be the usual top; my sister wanted something bold. She suggested a cape
  or billowing sleeves but I convinced her to go with imitation pearls draped across her back. This decision reduced fabric
  needed to create the back, and also made a statement piece in and of itself. With the counterweight of the pearls, that
  were made to be detachable from the dress, the top added that draping effect she wanted and paired well with her acessories.
